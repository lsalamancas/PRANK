\section*{Abstract}

\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\numberline{}Abstract}
\graphicspath{{images/}}

Human gait is a fundamental component of mobility, autonomy, and overall quality of life. Among the joints involved in locomotion, the ankle plays a crucial role in absorbing shock during heel strike and facilitating propulsion during the push-off phase. Impairments in ankle function — whether due to neurological conditions such as stroke, orthopaedic trauma, or post-surgical recovery — can significantly compromise gait, balance, and independence. Robotic rehabilitation has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional therapy, offering consistent, repeatable, and quantitatively measurable support.

This project proposes the design and computational simulation of PRANK (Parallel Robot for ANKle rehabilitation), a three-degree-of-freedom parallel robotic platform actuated by an RRR electric motor configuration and controlled via an admittance algorithm based on real-time force feedback. The work encompasses the complete mechanical design and CAD modelling in SolidWorks with finite element analysis and kinematic workspace verification; the full electronic architecture specification — including motor drivers, a six-axis force/torque sensor, and a dual-core STM32 embedded controller; and the implementation and validation of the admittance control firmware through hardware-in-the-loop testing, in which a physical STM32 development board runs the production firmware while exchanging sensor and actuator data with a ROS~2/Gazebo computational simulation acting as the virtual plant.
